## 1. Decisions

**D1 — The DS layout engine is the production layout authority now.** `[owner]` « 1 + on planifie le recodage de tous les algo d elk » (18/10). Focus consumes the DS engine (L-R1..L-R11); it does not grow a second placer. Today h2a still renders scenes with `interim-nodes.js` and SvelteFlow `smoothstep` (`minZoom` 0.15) and never calls the DS engine `[both]`; closing that seam is Lot H2, gated by Lot DS2 (the DS packages are still `private:true`, without a published renderer/framework surface).

**D2 — Full ELK capability is re-implemented as our own JS, from free/open-licence or public algorithms.** `[owner]` « Les algo open licence libre ou algo public mais dans notre js a nous — prend le temps de developper la competences sur la comprehension fine des usages et des algos qui manqueraient. On respect mais on libere au max et on a nos propres souches. » (18/10 ~10:20). Consequences: (a) the programme is competence-building ordered by usages and by which algorithms are actually missing, not a checkbox race to 21 ids calling one solver; (b) each capability is an independently authored implementation ("nos propres souches") specified from publications; (c) licences are respected while the delivered code stays maximally free (the DS publishes under MIT; provenance is qualified before publication). The pinned inventory of §3 remains the completeness denominator `[both]`.

**D3 — Clean-room provenance is mandatory.** `[both]` No ELK source, generated JS, comments, or fixtures are read, copied, translated, or fed to a model for "reformulation" in implementation work. Each family gets: a behavioural spec written from publications (variables, objective, constraints, tie-breaks, stopping rules), original fixtures, a provenance log, and a reviewer distinct from the implementer. A black-box comparison against a correctly licensed ELK, run outside the shipped runtime with version/hash/licence branch recorded, is allowed as measurement only; its output is never the code and never a bit-identity target. Unpublished implementation details are decided and documented as our own choices, never presented as recovered ELK behaviour.

**D4 — Geometry belongs to the layout optimizer, never to a skill or an author.** `[owner]` « c'est très perturbant quand les flèches ne sont pas branchées au centre des diagrammes quand c'est possible … ce n'est pas à un skill de faire ça mais à un layout optimizer ». Authors and skills provide semantics (`scene` without layout hints, per the 17/10 DS/Focus split), references, scenarios, and evidence. Ports, slots, routes, and labels are computed. No `FRAME_PLAN`, faces, or coordinates are authored in `scene` `[both]`.

**D5 — Geometric quality is engineered, then audited on the final painted scene.** `[owner]` « Insuffisamment etudié. Y a des algo pour ca dans elk... ca ressemble a de l amateurisme ». The engine prevents defects through the algorithm phases of §3/§4 and proves the result on the complete painted geometry (cards, titles, labels, stroke width, arrowheads, ports, camera). Hard invariants are at zero (§4.1); soft objectives are minimized under a versioned policy. A result carrying an unresolved hard diagnostic is not presented as qualified `[both]`.

**D6 — Every diagram is a graph; every representation is a synthesis; openable nesting is a complementary option.** `[owner]` « Avoir des diagrammes gigogne (ouvrables) pour les complexités est aussi une bonne facon. Tout diagramme est un graph et toute representation est une forme de synthese. Il faut bien modeliser diagramme layout et skills de presentation. » (18/10 ~10:20). Model proposed in §2 `[prop]`, grounded in that requirement and the two analyses: one semantic graph; named synthesis projections (overview, scenario, before/after state, collapsed group) derived from it with traceable, reversible mappings; layout computed per projection; presentation skills compose projections and evidence. Nesting (gigogne) is one attested good way to handle complexity (« aussi une bonne facon »); it is a candidate synthesis for complex diagrams, qualified per usage (§4, §5), without any universal priority over the complementary principles of D7. Miniaturising a whole scene below the readability floor remains non-conforming (§4.1).

**D7 — Two complementary design-system principles apply, selected by usage.** `[owner]` « 1 et 2 sont de bons principes. … La tu mets deux principes de type design system complementaires selon les usages » (18/10 ~10:20). `[gap]` The two numbered principles are not reproduced verbatim in the available inputs, so this spec does not name them. What is normative from the verbatim: the two principles are complementary, not competing, and the choice between them is driven by usage. Enumerating them is OQ3; no lot may hard-code a single presentation principle for all usages in the meantime.

**D8 — One primary process explorer per execution dossier.** `[owner]` (`OWNER-FEEDBACK-V3.md`, read in full by both analyses): DS-conforming heat map; ONE diagram whose clickable use-case paths highlight the concerned boxes; before/after toggle in that same diagram; groupable boxes with a `+` that can be zoomed; a plan diagram whose clickable steps highlight the modified elements, with time benefit visible at each step. §5 distributes each piece to its owner (DS component, `diagram-core`/engine, Focus kit, sentropic data). Separate per-case diagrams (including a separate "logo" diagram) are removed; the logo case becomes a scenario of the main diagram or disappears `[owner]`.

**D9 — `focus build` is extended immediately to heavy-evidence dossiers.** `[owner]` « Étendre focus build tout de suite ». Sources, citations, and annexes become first-class, versioned Focus contract (§6), independent of new layout solvers. The earlier "heavy dossier = standalone kit" exception is superseded `[both]`.

**D10 — Reactivity and atomicity of edits outrank byte size; delivery is dual-mode (CDN or embedded), offline always possible.** `[owner]` « Les diagrammes (graphes) doivent etre rendus par des lib de facon reactives. L enjeu n est pas la taille mais la reactivite et l atomicite des editions pour aller rapidement pour les petits et gros dossiers. Ok pour 32Mo mais logiquement les libs compilees et dispo sur cdn devraient tres largement reduire la taille. Et la capacite offline sans cdn doit etre possible donc ok la 32Mo qd js embed » (18/10 ~10:20). Attested owner properties `[owner]`: diagrams render through reactive libraries; edits stay reactive and atomic on small and large dossiers alike; compiled libraries on CDN reduce delivery size, and fully embedded offline consultation remains possible (≈32 MB accepted); byte size is not the acceptance criterion. Proposed implementation contract `[prop]`: equivalent dossier semantics in the two modes; the edit unit, the atomically published state, and revision checks of §2.1; bounded incremental recomputation as a candidate way to meet latency budgets while preserving camera/selection. The consumer's current recreate-on-`scene`-change path resets selection; it needs correction against these properties, not rejection merely for its choice of algorithm. A different mechanism that demonstrably meets the same latency budgets **and** the §2.1 atomicity contract may replace incremental recomputation; bounded cost alone never counts as atomicity. Latency and atomicity are measured and gated per increment (§6.5, §8); byte figures are reported, not used to refuse evidence. H1 delivers embedded evidence independently; H2 adds the DS/CDN wiring, H3 the linked interactions, and H4 qualifies each delivered increment (§7/§8).

**D11 — Focus contract versioning: v1 immutable, new capabilities behind an explicit discriminant.** `[both]` for the principle; `[prop]` for the exact discriminant name and number (V1). The new builder accepts v1 unchanged and the new version; unknown versions are refused before any output is replaced. No forward compatibility is promised: an old consumer rejects new content explicitly. The published v1 schema file and URN are never edited retroactively.

**D12 — No silent loss, anywhere.** `[both]` Unsupported algorithm options, unsupported evidence, unplaced required labels, and relations hidden by a projection are reported explicitly with references. Nothing is dropped, truncated, or silently re-routed to an unrelated algorithm to make a gate pass. Search exhaustion is reported as exhaustion, never as proven infeasibility.

**D13 — Business semantics and the time model belong to the consumer.** `[both]` sentropic owns CI/CD dependencies, conditions, measured/estimated/unknown durations, and plan benefits. The DS owns layout and interaction primitives. h2a/Focus owns the dossier contract, compilation, and portability. The engine never infers that branches are alternatives, that one gate input suffices, or that a transitive edge is redundant.

**D14 — PR #696 is closed and is not a baseline.** `[both]` The fresh plan starts from a measured baseline. No lot may present #696 acceleration as done or in progress.

**D15 — Before/after, case, event and group controls belong inside the diagram.** `[owner]` (18/10, relayed by s-conductor, capture 20): « aucun sens du design. les boutons sont à 2 km, t'as pas utilisé notre générateur de diagramme. Mets le bouton (DANS le diagramme) : avant/après, cas, événement, options de groupes ». These four control families are part of the reusable DS renderer contract (§5), bound by Focus to the consumer's declared states; distant page controls do not satisfy the request. This completes D8 without changing the same-canvas or exploration-only requirements. The inventory does not establish which existing generator the owner meant by « notre »; mutualisation must bridge the existing capital rather than attribute that word to a particular package (§2.2).

**D16 — Mutualise the generators and capitalise this dossier through three explicit layers.** `[owner]` (18/10, relayed by s-conductor): « demande à diagramme de mutualiser, car ils sont pas si mal que ça à part les flèches mal centrées, je voulais déjà savoir. capitalise ce dossier pour une bonne segmentation modèle graph engram / modèle de layout et rendu / style. » Graph-model guardianship belongs to engram, consistent with the owner direction « Il doit etre le gardien de toutes les ontologies de dev » recorded by the ontology-guardian brainstorm. ## 2. Three layers: graph model / layout and rendering / style

The ownership segmentation of D16 has **three layers**. The v2 functional distinctions — semantic graph, synthesis, layout, presentation — remain: synthesis and visual interaction are part of the DS layout/rendering layer; Focus composes that renderer with dossier sections and evidence. Dossier composition does not create a fourth diagram model or give Focus a second geometric engine.

| Layer | Content | Owner | Forbidden content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Graph model | Entities, relations, semantic containment (`contains`), stable identities, profile types/roles/constraints. `SemanticDocument generic@1` is the existing contract; richer profiles require stable reusable semantic invariants. | **engram**, guardian of definitions/ontologies; current implementation capital is DS `diagram-core`. | Coordinates, port geometry, camera/control state, colours or theme-token values. No second node/edge schema. |
| 2 — Layout and rendering | Traceable synthesis projections (overview, scenario with explicit entity **and** relation sets and parallel branches, before/after, plan delta, collapsed groups with member mappings); computed ranking, ordering, placement, ports, routes, labels and camera; scene, hit-testing and **in-canvas controls**. Deterministic, reactive, atomically published (§2.1); incremental computation remains `[prop]`. | **design-system**: current core view/transaction machinery, `diagram-canvas` headless engine, reusable renderer/components. | New business semantics or invented dependencies; silent omission; author coordinates or skill-supplied arrow tweaks; executable author callbacks or approval on exploration clicks. |
| 3 — Style | Theme identity and DS token mappings for typography, colour, icons, emphasis and control appearance. The graph supplies typed semantic categories; theme tokens and actual fonts supply rendering/measurement inputs. | **design-system** themes/components; Focus selects/hosts the theme. | Geometry or arrow-centre repairs hidden in CSS; colours/positions embedded as semantic facts; contrast or readability bypasses. |

Guardianship is a target responsibility, not a claim that code already moved. The brainstorm leaves open whether definitions alone or the whole `diagram-core` package transfer, and where shared command/view machinery resides. Preserve its types, validation, transactions, identities and existing #368 output; reuse pinned/versioned profiles offline without a runtime MCP call on the rendering path. The transfer mechanism must be reconciled in that concertation, without blocking DS adoption or inventing a parallel graph format. Focus retains its dossier contract, compilation, evidence and portability (D9/D11/D13); presentation skills guide composition and reading order through the shared renderer, without authoring geometry.

Two distinctions are load-bearing `[both]`: semantic containment ≠ visual grouping (frame-profile capital, r4/r4.1); a visual bundle ≠ a semantic join ≠ a hyperedge. The frame profile is reused as a placement/composition capital, not as a new business profile and not as an authoring format.

### 2.1 Edit unit and atomic publication

The properties are owner requirements (D10); the following operational contract and mechanisms are `[prop]`. A **semantic edit** is one accepted `diagram-core` transaction against an explicit `baseRevision`, potentially containing several commands. A **presentation transition** is one local intent (scenario, event, snapshot, step, expand/collapse, viewport/measurement change); it changes no business graph by itself and carries its own monotonically ordered request token. A **dossier rebuild** is one validated input envelope, including evidence bytes and citations, producing one output artifact. These are distinct units; exploration never becomes a semantic mutation or an approval.

Reuse existing capital at DS `25c0f3d…`: `packages/diagram-core/src/commands.ts` (`applyTransaction`, `baseRevision`, `revision-conflict`) already validates a working copy, refuses without mutating the caller, and publishes a single new document/view revision. Existing `tests/commands-inverse.test.ts` explicitly covers a stale revision, one refused command, and an invalid final state. Those guarantees are retained; they do **not** yet prove asynchronous layout or browser publication atomic. No competing semantic transaction system is introduced.

The presentation publishes one consistent tuple: semantic input hash/revision, projection and local-state token, measurement/viewport version, computed view/routes/labels, quality status/report, and corresponding selection/expansion/camera. A candidate is prepared and validated off the displayed state. Publication checks that **all** tokens still match the latest requested state, then replaces the tuple as one observable commit. While pending, the last complete scene remains identified as such; requested controls must not label it as the completed new state. A failure keeps the previous tuple intact and reports the cause. Camera/selection are retained where references survive, with explicit removed/not-applicable handling otherwise. Evidence content, digest, locator resolution, and rendered citation target likewise belong to one dossier revision. No mixed-revision frame is accepted, even if every individual task meets its time budget.

Concurrent requests are tested by completing an older slow result after a newer one; stale results, obsolete font/viewport measurements, and cancelled work cannot overwrite the latest state. Inject a failure after layout but before label/quality completion, and a refusal in the middle of a semantic transaction: neither may leak partial state. H1 separately proves failed validation/build leaves the previous HTML byte-identical and publishes no half-written replacement. DS4/H2/H3 prove presentation integration; DS6b adds asynchronous solver/stability cases. Pure, component and browser witnesses are assigned separately in §7.4. Bounded incremental recomputation and displacement objectives remain proposed performance/stability mechanisms, never a substitute for these publication assertions.

## 3. ELK-capability programme (design-system)

### 3.1 Denominator and reading rules

The programme covers every entry of the pinned catalogue inventory, at three distinct levels `[both]`: (1) placement algorithms; (2) phases, strategies, and intermediate processors they expose; (3) provider/adapter entries, whose observable capability is covered without copying their runtimes or Eclipse interfaces. The catalogue snapshot is not certified identical to any single ELK tag; Lot DS1 freezes the reference (version, documents, hashes) and tracks drift. The number 21 is the pinned denominator, not a permanent ceiling. The five Graphviz entries exposed by ELK are in scope; the other Graphviz engines of the general DS catalogue stay in their own existing programme and are neither erased nor silently added to this denominator. Coverage states, per entry and per strategy: implemented behaviour, constraints, source publications, test evidence, limitations. Provider coverage never implies binary, plugin, API, or byte-for-byte compatibility (OQ1).

## 4. Geometric quality: prevent, measure, then present

Evidence anchor: owner screenshots 8/11/13 (fan of 20–30 edges onto `ci-gate`, edges crossing unrelated cards, nodes cut at the left edge, labels under edges, unusable before/after). They are visual/functional regression cases. A screenshot alone does not prove semantic edge counts or whether cropping came from initial fit or manual pan; fixtures are derived from the source graph plus the reproduced interaction state `[both]`. Purchase (1997) and Huang (2008) support prioritising crossings and crossing angle for reading tasks; neither supplies a universal numeric threshold, a global score, or a minimum font size. Every number below is therefore either a geometric invariant or a versioned product policy `[both]`.

The audited scene is the final painted scene: card and title rectangles, labels, full paths, stroke width, arrowheads, ports, camera transform. Splines are audited by curve intersection or bounded-error approximation — never by control polygon alone. Collisions are measured in world units, perception in effective CSS pixels. Geometric tolerances are documented and scale-relative (`1e-6` world, ≤ 1 px CSS as measurement tolerances `[prop]`); raster tolerance (≈ ½ CSS px, antialiasing) is a separate test concern. No tolerance may hide several pixels of cut text.

### 4.2 The `ci-gate` hub, treated preventively

Neither centring 30 arrows on one point nor unbounded growth is a solution: 30 slots at `(n+1) × 12` already need a 372-unit side before labels `[both]`. A star graph is planar — this case can be bad at zero crossings. Chain:

1. The consumer supplies the real dependencies, job families, and gate semantics. The engine invents nothing (D13).
2. Ordering clusters related branches, reserves a corridor before the hub, and orders slots by source order to reduce inversions.
3. Independent branches keep distinct, symmetrically distributed attachments. A bundle entry, if used, is centred; the aggregate keeps the exact list of represented relations.
4. A collapsed test group projects its boundary relations as traceable aggregates with an intelligible count; `+` restores branches and labels under the same references.
5. A scenario click highlights the concerned branches and expands their detail on demand; off-scenario context stays readable instead of forming an opaque mass.
6. The engine may retry ranking/ports/routing, then change detail level (D6 synthesis). It never succeeds by deleting gate inputs.

Visual bundle, semantic join ("all these checks must pass"), and hyperedge are three different concepts and are never conflated `[both]`. Any transitive reduction is explicit and consumer-qualified: in CI, a direct edge can carry a contract even when another chain connects the same jobs. Intentional cycles and self-loops stay legal and routed; a global `source === target` ban fixes nothing and breaks the DS contract.

### 4.3 Optimise, then diagnose — with stated limits

The engine builds bounded deterministic candidates (LR/TB; longest-path / NS / width-aware ranking; barycentre / median + local improvement; PAVA / BK / NS placement; then routing and labels), audits each candidate's painted geometry, retries the implicated phases, and keeps the best **admissible** candidate `[both]`. Selection is lexicographic: semantic and geometric invariants first, then mode readability and stability, then crossings, bends/detours, and frame use. A weighted score never compensates a collision with good fill. Today's "LR fits in width, therefore LR" (L-R5) is not this optimisation. Changing the L-R3 router cost (today bends-first on fixed docks/grid) requires amending and versioning that policy and its tests — the two contracts are never presented as equivalent.

Feasibility evidence reuses DS `tools/elk-corpus-measure/` (README, `measure.mjs`, `evidence/`): with `s_max ≤ sqrt(A_frame / A_cards)` on disjoint card surfaces, an upper bound below the required zoom **proves** the full-detail overview cannot fit; a sufficient bound proves nothing. Diagnostics stay distinct: precise violation (references + coordinates); search budget exhausted (no admissible candidate found — not infeasibility); contradictory constraints or proven area infeasibility; detail view required (lossless collapsed projection proposed); unsupported capability (id + migration need). Search budgets are deterministic (iteration/work limits) plus an operational time guard. Every result records algorithm version, options, seed, metrics, text-measurement profile, viewport content size, camera, and projection state, so it is reproducible.

Control runs at three levels `[both]`: build-time data prevalidation; pure DS computation under a declared measurement profile; DOM qualification of the final portable with real fonts, dimensions, and interactions. A headless preflight does not attest every browser. `layoutDocument` may return `laid-out` with `route-obstructed` / `label-unplaced` / `label-budget-exhausted` / `row-fill-partial`; for dossier presentation those unresolved diagnostics are not success, and the renderer must not announce the scene as qualified. The renderer replays the DS-exported `relationRoute` and `placeEdgeLabels` (label rectangles are derived from view + measurements, not stored in `ViewDocument`); Focus grows no second label heuristic. The internal centre segment `buildScene` may add for entity-borne endpoints is painted under the box; it is neither a foreign crossing nor the real attachment.

Normative summary `[both]`: layout success and presentation readiness are separate outcomes. Presentation readiness requires semantic coverage, resolved diagnostics, and zero hard violations on the final rendered projection. The engine attempts deterministic repairs within a declared budget before reporting failure, and reports proven constraints, measured scores, exhausted searches, and unverified host behaviour as different facts. A quality report binds semantic input hash, engine/policy versions, options/seed, measurement profile, viewport, camera, per-metric results, and offending references; browser qualification additionally binds the rendered artifact hash and the exercised state matrix. A report for another artifact or matrix is not acceptance evidence.

## 6. `focus build` extension for heavy-evidence dossiers (h2a)

### 6.1 Observed limits

`[both]` `packages/focus/src/model.ts` + `schemas/decision-dossier-document.v1.schema.json` define a closed document (`1.0.0`) with six section types: `prose`, `question`, `optionSet`, `outcome`, `amendmentTrace`, `diagram`. No annex/source/citation contract exists; the validator rebuilds allowed objects, so adding JSON fields or renderer code alone achieves nothing. `packages/focus/src/validation/readability.ts` budgets the decision path (2/8/30 minutes by stakes at 180 words/minute, ≤ 2 minutes per question) and flags undefined codes — pasting workflows, logs, or diffs into `prose` explodes the budget. `packages/h2a/src/focus-build.ts` additionally restricts `generic@1` ad hoc (today ≤ 80 entities, ≤ 240 relations; non-empty ports/resources/extensions refused). Those graph limits are not annex-size limits. `DiagramNode.scene` is an opaque `Record` at model level. Conclusion: the change spans model, validation, reading budget, renderer, and distribution together.

### 6.2 Contract (first increment, independent of new solvers)

Names below are the proposed contract `[prop]`; only the versioning discipline (D11) is `[both]`. The evidence increment ships before the typed interaction bindings of §5; annexes do not wait for the explorer.

| Element | Content and invariants | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Version discriminant | Explicit, dispatched before closed validation. Absence = legacy 1.0 per a documented rule. Unknown version refused. Not to be confused with `scene.schemaVersion` (diagram-core). Exact field/number frozen in Lot H1 (V1). | Focus model + validator. |
| `sources` | Catalogue by stable id: title, origin (repo/revision/path or frozen URI), media type, encoding, embedded bytes, SHA-256 over the **original bytes**, size. A source is stored once and referenced everywhere. Provenance fields are data — never paths read or URLs fetched at opening time. | Focus integrity contract; consumer owns authenticity and selection. |
| `citations` | Id → source + typed locator: inclusive line range for text (line convention defined once and tested: CRLF, trailing empty line, Unicode), whole-image reference + caption for raster captures (no fake lines). Human-readable label. Unique ids; every reference and range resolves. Bindable to a section, question, option, diagram reference, or plan step. | Pure validation + evidence navigation. |
| Evidence groups | Short summary + list of citations/sources backing one claim, without unrolling every source. | Focus. |

First-increment media `[both]`: UTF-8 text, logs, YAML/JSON/diffs, structured textual annexes; PNG/JPEG captures as inert binary evidence with digest, dimensions, alt text. An accepted-but-unrendered type stays identified and downloadable as inert bytes; unaccepted types are refused explicitly. Rich rendering (PDF, active SVG) is a later, separately announced capability — never executed author content. Byte fidelity: digests cover original bytes; JSON/HTML escaping and decoded display never alter the restituted bytes; line endings are preserved for citations; nothing is normalised after hashing. A hash proves byte identity — not claim truth, not provenance authenticity, not owner approval. Hostile JS/HTML inside a source stays inert text; the existing `jsonForHtml` script-closure protection stays mandatory. Embedded bytes are the mandatory path; the optional build-time resolver of V4, if ever added, resolves allowlisted references before packaging and never auto-discovers files. The delivered HTML is autonomous offline (embedded mode, D10) with no local path needed by the reader.

Scale evidence `[both]`: the spec-review kit already holds 31 sources / 813 567 text bytes; the CI portable is ≈14 MB. Budgets are therefore set in bytes and render cost, measured on the real corpus — never an opportunistic "25 sources" cap. Numeric refusal thresholds are `[prop]` (V2), refused explicitly with the largest contributors named, never met by truncation.

### 6.3 Reading budget without degrading the decision

`[both]` The readability rules keep applying to the complete primary decision path: literal request, stakes, real question, comparable options, recommendation, decision-changing risks, conclusions, and evidence summaries. Raw source bytes and supporting annex detail get separate, disclosed volume accounting (source count, bytes, estimated consultation time). Raw sources skip the editorial acronym check; annex titles, summaries, and visible explanations do not. Collapsing decision content never exempts it. A new option, a choice-changing risk, or a material reservation must surface in the primary path even when its demonstration is long. "Two minutes of reading" is never claimed for a multi-thousand-line corpus. The schema cannot prove a material risk was hidden in an annex — typing and UI make the bypass visible; the rest stays author/review responsibility, stated as such.

### 6.4 Pipeline, rendering, compatibility

Pipeline `[both]`: recognise version → validate pure structure and references → resolve explicit build pieces if any → verify bytes/digests/ranges → compile scenes and bindings → apply primary-path budget → compose bundle → write output atomically only on success. A refused validation leaves any previous output untouched and names the offending path/source/citation. Renderer: annex/source index, open-from-citation, numbered lines, back-to-context, in-source search, raw download; long sources render lazily in bounded DOM (no `fetch(file://…)`), summary first. JSON parse cost and duplicated data are measured even when panels start collapsed (D10 reactivity).

Geometry honesty `[both]`: the build never claims to have validated a render it did not measure. DS adoption defines a measurement provider bound to the actually embedded fonts/tokens, computes the reference view under the renderer's constraints, and browser tests verify correspondence with loaded fonts and the real viewport. While build-time measurement remains approximate during migration, reports say so; the browser re-evaluates on dimension/font change and never presents a scene known invalid. No browser download is imposed on the portable's reader.

| Situation | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| v1 document, new build | Accepted under its historical rules; same request, sections, hash meaning, affordances. No artificial annexes required. Existing `template` stays available; a new-version template is selected explicitly. |
| New-version document, new build | Sources/annexes validated; rendered offline with resolved citations and a separate primary-path budget. |
| New-version document, old build | Explicit version/field refusal. No compatibility promised in this direction; evidence is never stripped to fake success. |
| Markdown/terminal export | Readable references/annex index at minimum, or an explicit capability diagnostic. No silent drop via an incomplete switch. |
| Legacy scene + fallback source | Previously supported formats keep working; source and alt stay consultable; the compiler produces derived representations. |
| `comprehensionEvidence` present | Existing binding to document hash and authority respected. Compilation never fabricates a fresh attestation; a substantive change never recycles an old approval. Snapshot `hash`/`cursor` provenance preserved; extended-envelope, evidence, and HTML fingerprints computed separately. |
| Rebuild from the embedded document | References and contents identical; if the rendering environment changed, its provenance and quality report change explicitly. A report from the old HTML is never reused. |

The current builder graph limits (1–80 entities, ≤ 240 relations; empty ports/resources/extensions) do not disappear when sources are added. The CI dossier (67 jobs, groups, variants) may need an adapted projection; `contains` edges and variants are inventoried before claiming the bounds hold. Any widening is specified and tested separately. Embedded copies of schema/validator under `packages/h2a/focus-kit` are regenerated from `packages/focus` with their fingerprints (existing documented chain) — bypassing it would leave a CLI validating a different format than the renderer. Package versioning follows real impact: a TypeScript union addition can break exhaustive consumers; the schema number alone guarantees no non-breaking release.

### 6.5 H1 exit gate: measured evidence consultation on small and large dossiers

H1 cannot be accepted on schema acceptance and a successful click alone. G5a is an H1 release condition, independent of DS solvers, CDN delivery and H3 interactions. The following protocol is `[prop]`, implementing D10. Before qualification, H1 freezes `packages/h2a/focus-kit/test/fixtures/evidence-performance-policy.v1.json` (new): numeric budgets, corpus/generator hashes, original and packaged bytes, source/line counts, viewport/font/theme profiles, pinned Node/Chromium/library versions, runner CPU/RAM, repetition counts and memory measurement method. Initial exploratory measurements set the numeric values; the recorded policy is frozen **before** the RED/GREEN qualification run and is never loosened to turn a failing run green. Missing budgets, unavailable instrumentation or an absent fixture fail the gate. No universal millisecond or memory limit is invented in this spec.

Corpus `[prop]`: a small legacy fixture plus a small new-format dossier (three sources, short text and a capture), and a large new-format dossier with >25 sources, at least one 20 000-line UTF-8/CRLF source, distant citations, a capture, and a short primary decision path. A fully embedded large artifact around the owner-accepted 32 MB is included; its evidence/runtime byte split is reported, not treated as a size cap. The same long-source fixture with twice as many lines checks DOM growth independently of input size. Freeze the real corpus references used for calibration; synthetic line repetition alone does not qualify the real corpus. All inputs, generated HTML, browser profiles, XDG/config/state roots and reports are copied/generated under `mkdtemp`.

| Property | Measurement and pass condition fixed before qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Cold opening | Thirty independent fresh browser contexts with caches/service workers absent, network disabled, and HTML opened from the generated local file; from navigation start to a usable primary view and evidence index. Record every sample, p50/p95/max and compare the frozen budgets on both sizes. Embedded parse/decode work is included even for collapsed annexes. |
| Citation access | Thirty deterministic navigations, including a citation near the end of the 20 000-line source and a return to context. From activation to the correct numbered range becoming visible and focusable; p95/max under policy, source bytes/range verified. No H3 scenario/plan state is required in H1. |
| Search | Thirty fixed queries covering early/late hits, Unicode, repeated hits and no result. Input to correct visible result/count, with cancellation/replacement by a newer query; p95/max under policy and no stale result published. Search covers the complete source, not just the rendered window. |
| Bounded DOM | Count rendered source lines and total evidence DOM nodes on open, distant jump, scroll and close/reopen. A fixed window/overscan budget bounds rendered lines/nodes for both the 20 000-line source and its doubled counterpart; total input length must not cause every line to enter the DOM. |
| Memory | Measure peak browser memory and retained memory after the navigation/search cycles using the frozen method on browser processes spawned by the harness only (including workers), plus JS heap where supported. Record initial/peak/retained values, instrumentation limitations and the policy's allowed growth. Evidence bytes may scale with corpus size; unbounded retention across repeated consultation is a failure. |

Proposed targeted witness: `packages/h2a/focus-kit/test/evidence-performance-browser.test.mjs` (§7.4), separately from functional `evidence-browser.test.mjs`. It records artifact/input/policy hashes, exact command, environment, exit code, sample output and raw metrics with the release evidence. The same declared browsers and 320/390/1440 px viewports exercise the usability paths; instrumentation may use pinned Chromium, explicitly without claiming other-browser memory coverage. A feature refusal is a functional RED, not a measured latency result. Any performance regression needs a behavioural/budget assertion on an executable path, never an import failure. H1 also passes the build-publication failure assertions of §2.1/G7; passing speed budgets alone does not prove atomicity.
